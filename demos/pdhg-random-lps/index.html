The random LPs

Standard form $\min\, c^\top x$ s.t. $Ax=b,\ x\ge 0$, from the model in the experiments of [1, 2]: $A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ has i.i.d. $N(0,1)$ entries (or $\pm1$ entries); $\hat x=(|u_1|,\dots,|u_m|,0,\dots,0)$ and $\hat s=(0,\dots,0,|u_{m+1}|,\dots,|u_n|)$ with $u\sim N(0,I)$; $b=A\hat x$ and $c=\hat s+A^\top\hat y$, with $\hat y$ chosen so that $Ac=0$. Then $x^\star=\hat x$ and $s^\star=\hat s$ are known, so $\Phi$ follows from its closed form.

Each dot is one random LP

    rPDHG, the stopping rule and what [1] proves

    Each LP is solved by rPDHG (Algorithm 1 of [1, 2], as on the one-LP page) from $0$ until $\|(x,s)-(x^\star,s^\star)\|_2\le 10^{-4}$, as in the experiments of [2]. A run stops after $\min\{10^6,\max\{10^5,\ 4\cdot10^9/(mn)\}\}$ iterations; such LPs are hollow dots.

    With probability at least $1-\delta$, rPDHG finds the optimal basis within $\widetilde O(n^{2.5}m^{0.5}/\delta)$ iterations ($\widetilde O(n^{2.5}/\delta)$ for Gaussian $A$), and then an $\varepsilon$-optimal solution within $O\big(\frac{\sqrt{nm}}{\delta}\ln\frac1\varepsilon\big)$ more [1]. This holds for sub-Gaussian $A$ with $n/(n-m+1)$ bounded and $\delta$ not exponentially small.

    What makes some LPs slow?

      $\Phi$ and the iteration bound of [2]

      With a unique optimum, rPDHG needs at most $O\big(\kappa\Phi\ln(\kappa\Phi\|w^\star\|/\varepsilon)\big)$ iterations [2], where $w^\star=(x^\star,s^\star)$, $\kappa$ is the condition number of $A$, and, with the optimal basis $B$, the other indices $N$ and $T=B^{-1}N$,

      \[\Phi=\big(\|x^\star\|_1+\|s^\star\|_1\big)\cdot\max\Big\{\max_j\frac{\sqrt{\|T_{:,j}\|^2+1}}{s^\star_{N_j}},\ \max_i\frac{\sqrt{\|T_{i,:}\|^2+1}}{x^\star_{B_i}}\Big\}.\]
      \[\begin{aligned}\Phi=\big(\|x^\star\|_1+\|s^\star\|_1\big)\cdot\max\Big\{&\max_j\frac{\sqrt{\|T_{:,j}\|^2+1}}{s^\star_{N_j}},\\ &\max_i\frac{\sqrt{\|T_{i,:}\|^2+1}}{x^\star_{B_i}}\Big\}.\end{aligned}\]

      The bounds of [1] and [2] are $O(\cdot)$ bounds, so the dashed line shows only their slope.
