Zikai Xiong

Assistant Professor, Industrial Engineering and Management Sciences, Northwestern University

I am a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the Northwestern University Department of Industrial Engineering and Management Sciences (IEMS). I received my Ph.D. from the MIT Operations Research Center (ORC), advised by Prof. Robert Freund. Before joining Northwestern, I spent a year as a postdoctoral fellow at the Georgia Tech H. Milton Stewart School of Industrial and Systems Engineering (ISyE), working with Prof. Katya Scheinberg. My research interests are primarily in optimization, with twin interests in theoretical foundations and computational practice.

For students: I am always interested in working with motivated students. Northwestern students and prospective PhD students who are interested in my research are welcome to reach out. PhD admission is handled through the IEMS PhD program.

Research Overview

view publications →

My research is in optimization theory and computation. Two main lines of my current work are large-scale optimization on AI-era computing hardware and optimization with humans and AI. A recurring goal in my work is to uncover insights into algorithmic behavior that inform both theory and computational practice. I am delighted that some of my methods have been implemented in state-of-the-art commercial optimization solvers. My work has received second place in the George B. Dantzig Dissertation Award, won the INFORMS Optimization Society Student Paper Prize, and been a finalist in the George Nicholson Student Paper Competition. My overarching aspiration is to broaden the capabilities of optimization to solve problems arising in operations research, machine learning, and AI.
